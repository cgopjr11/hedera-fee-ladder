## Hedera in about ninety seconds

HBAR is the native token of the Hedera network. It pays transaction fees, funds smart contract execution, covers file storage, and gets staked to help secure the network. Without a little HBAR sitting in an account, you can't do much on Hedera at all — same role ETH plays on Ethereum.

The network itself isn't a blockchain. ## Spot, Convert, or the leveraged perpetual

Three ways to end up holding HBAR on Gate, and they are not interchangeable.

Convert is the beginner route: enter the HBAR amount, confirm, done. You pay a spread instead of a commission, and you don't get to set a price.

The HBAR/USDT spot pair is the standard route. Limit order costs less, market order is faster. If your plan is to accumulate over months, limit orders set slightly below the current price will often fill during normal volatility and cost you the maker rate instead of the taker rate.

The HBAR/USDT perpetual contract is a different instrument. It uses leverage, and leveraged positions can be liquidated. If the goal is "own some HBAR," a perpetual is the wrong tool. It makes sense only if you're actively trading, and anyone using it should read the futures fee schedule on the platform's own fee page rather than trusting a secondhand number.

## Getting HBAR off the exchange

Coins held on an exchange are an IOU, not custody. If you're holding more than you'd shrug off losing, withdraw.

Hedera addresses come in two forms: an account ID like `0.0.x`, and a `0x...` address for EVM-compatible wallets. Your destination wallet will tell you which one it expects. Two rules that prevent the most common loss:

- Some exchanges and wallets require a memo or memo field when withdrawing to certain account types. Send a small test amount first. On-chain transfers can't be reversed once confirmed.
- Make sure the network you select matches the receiving wallet. Sending HBAR on the wrong network, or sending an HBAR-pegged token on an EVM chain to a Hedera-native address, doesn't end well.

Wallets people commonly pair with HBAR include HashPack, the official Hedera wallet, MyHbarWallet, and hardware options like Ledger and Trezor. Staking HBAR to a node is possible from a self-custody wallet, which is one more reason to move coins off an exchange if you're holding long term.

## Where this goes wrong

A short list of failure modes, all of them common.

**Wrong HBAR.** Search for HBAR on an EVM chain and you'll find wrapped or bridged tokens with similar tickers. Check the contract address against an official source before buying anything that isn't the native HBAR on Hedera.

**Denying yourself the first transaction.** Every action on Hedera costs a small amount of HBAR. Withdraw a balance exactly to zero and you can't do anything with it until you send more in.

**Ignoring where you live.** Gate's user agreement lists restricted territories, and the United States, Canada, Iran and Cuba appear on that list. Check your own jurisdiction before completing KYC. Payment methods also vary by region, and some won't appear for you at all.

**Assuming the card fee is small.** It's 1–5%, and it stacks on top of the trading commission. On a $500 purchase at 5%, the card alone costs $25.

**Leaving everything on the exchange forever.** Convenient, and fine for an amount you're actively trading. Not the same thing as holding.

## The risk side, stated plainly

HBAR is roughly 86% below its September 2021 high, and that number is not a reason to buy or a reason to avoid it — it's just what the chart says. What matters more:

- Of the 50 billion maximum supply, most is already circulating, but treasury and ecosystem allocations still exist and unlock schedules have historically weighed on price.
- Hedera's governance council model and its permissioned node structure draw criticism from people who prefer permissionless networks. Whether that's a flaw or a feature depends on whether you think enterprise adoption needs a known set of validators.
- It competes with a long list of smart contract platforms with larger developer bases.
- Crypto prices are volatile enough that short-term moves tell you very little. If a 30% drawdown would push you into a bad decision, size the position accordingly.

Nothing above is investment advice. It's the information you need to make the buy itself go smoothly, which is a separate question from whether you should.
